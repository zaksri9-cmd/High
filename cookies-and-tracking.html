Cookies, Tracking, and What a Website Can Learn

Cookies are only one part of online tracking. Understand common technologies and the choices that can meaningfully reduce unwanted data collection.

Afterglow Editorial•6 min read•Adults 18+

Cookies are only one part of online tracking. Understand common technologies and the choices that can meaningfully reduce unwanted data collection.

What a cookie is

A cookie is a small piece of data a website asks the browser to store. It may keep you signed in, remember a language preference, maintain a shopping cart, or help the operator measure usage. Some cookies are essential for a feature; others are used for analytics or advertising. Their purpose and lifetime depend on how the site is built.

Other ways sites measure activity

Websites may also use pixels, scripts, local storage, device and browser signals, server logs, or identifiers supplied by advertising partners. These tools can have different purposes and privacy implications. Deleting cookies can sign you out or clear preferences, but it does not necessarily remove records that a service already stored on its servers.

Choose settings with care

Review cookie controls when offered, and choose non-essential categories according to your preferences. Your browser may let you block third-party cookies, clear site data, or limit cross-site tracking. Blocking everything can break features, so test the settings and decide what balance works for you. A privacy policy should explain the major categories of data and the relevant choices available to visitors.

Advertising and privacy

Advertising systems can process information such as a page URL, coarse device information, or an approximate location inferred from network data. The exact data depends on the implementation and vendor. Publishers should follow applicable consent and transparency rules, particularly when serving visitors in regions with specific privacy requirements. Never claim a site collects no data unless you have verified every script and server component.

A practical approach

Keep software updated, limit unnecessary extensions, check app permissions, and avoid signing into accounts when you do not need to. Use the privacy controls that fit your needs, and remember that no single browser setting guarantees anonymity.
