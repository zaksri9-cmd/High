Design

What a Better Adult Website Experience Looks Like

A good website is easy to navigate, comfortable to read, and respectful of the visitor’s time. These design principles help adult and mainstream sites alike.

Afterglow Editorial•5 min read•Adults 18+

A good website is easy to navigate, comfortable to read, and respectful of the visitor’s time. These design principles help adult and mainstream sites alike.

Start with clear navigation

Visitors should be able to understand where they are and how to reach the main sections. Use descriptive menu labels, a predictable back path, and working internal links. Search and category filters are helpful only when they return relevant results and are easy to reset.

Design for phones first

Use responsive layouts, readable text, adequate spacing, and controls large enough to tap. Avoid horizontal scrolling, tiny close icons, and overlays that cover the entire screen. Test the site on a slower connection as well as a recent desktop browser.

Respect motion and attention

Autoplay video with sound can surprise people and use mobile data. Start media only after an intentional play action and provide visible pause and volume controls. Avoid animation that flashes or distracts from reading. Ads should not imitate site controls or trap the visitor in a navigation loop.

Make content accessible

Use meaningful headings, descriptive link text, sufficient color contrast, keyboard-accessible controls, and alternative text for informative images. Captions and transcripts can improve access when video or audio is present. Accessibility helps people using assistive technology and often improves usability for everyone.

Balance monetization and trust

An ad-supported site still needs to make its content easy to access. Use a small number of clearly separated ad placements and test their effect on page speed and layout. Avoid placing ads so close to navigation buttons that visitors are likely to click them accidentally. A visitor who can comfortably read and return is more valuable than one forced through interruptions.
