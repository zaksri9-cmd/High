Private Browsing: What It Does—and What It Does Not

Private browsing can be useful, but it is not an invisibility cloak. Learn what it saves, what it clears, and which other parties may still see activity.

Afterglow Editorial•6 min read•Adults 18+

Private browsing can be useful, but it is not an invisibility cloak. Learn what it saves, what it clears, and which other parties may still see activity.

What private mode usually changes

Most browsers offer a private, incognito, or InPrivate window. In general, the browser does not add that session’s history to its ordinary local browsing history, and it removes session cookies when all private windows are closed. This is useful when you share a device or do not want a particular session to remain in the normal history list.

Private mode does not make you anonymous. It does not automatically hide your IP address from websites, prevent your internet provider or employer from observing network activity, or stop a website from recognizing you after you sign in. Browser versions differ, so read the help information for the specific browser you use.

What it does not protect against

A private window does not make a downloaded file disappear, delete bookmarks you deliberately save, or erase information submitted to a website. A site can still collect information through account activity, server logs, cookies that are allowed during the session, and other browser technologies. Extensions may also have access to activity if the browser permits them in private mode.

If a device is managed by a school, employer, family safety product, or security software, its monitoring rules may still apply. Private mode is a local privacy feature, not a substitute for a safe device or a trustworthy network.

A sensible privacy routine

Close every private window when you finish. Avoid signing into personal accounts when you want a session to remain separate. Keep your browser and operating system updated, review extension permissions, and avoid downloading files from unfamiliar pages. Use a screen lock and a separate user profile on devices shared with other people.

A reputable VPN can change the network that sees your connection, but it does not make your identity invisible to websites you log into. A VPN provider may itself be able to observe connection metadata. Choose tools based on a clear threat model rather than marketing claims.

Quick checklist

Before browsing, ask whether the device is shared or managed, whether you need to sign into an account, and whether you trust the site. Afterwards, close the session and review any downloaded files. Do not use private mode as permission to ignore age rules, consent, copyright, or local law.
